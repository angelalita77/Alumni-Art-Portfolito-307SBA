My Artistic Journey

How It All Started (And Almost Didn't)

I picked up a pencil around age seven, mostly because I saw someone draw a cat that looked like an actual cat and thought, "I could do that." Reader, I could not do that. My early cats looked like potatoes with opinions. But I kept at it anyway, fueled by the pure, uninformed confidence that only a seven-year-old can have.

Then came the dark years. Every time I finished a drawing, I'd stare at it, compare it to literally anyone better than me — which was everyone — and quietly decide art wasn't for me. I'd put the sketchbook away for months, sometimes longer, convinced I'd peaked at "potato cat." This cycle repeated more times than I'd like to admit: draw, despair, disappear, repeat.

Eventually I realized the problem wasn't talent, it was patience — I kept expecting to skip the "bad at this" phase entirely. Once I accepted that improvement is just a thousand embarrassing drawings in a trench coat, things clicked. I stopped quitting every time a hand looked like a bunch of sausages, and slowly, drawing by drawing, the sausages started looking like fingers.
